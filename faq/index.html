Does the natural object in my hybrid monster have to be an animal?

No. The brief says "one of the objects should be artificially created, while the other should be a creature found in nature," and we are reading that more broadly: any natural object works, paired with a fabricated one. If you have already built yours around an animal, keep it.

Do the ten Methods 1 versions mean ten files?

No. The ten versions are laid out on one 11x17 sheet, submitted as a single file.

Do the ten Methods 1 versions have to be ten finished pieces?

No. They record the process. Some of them are trials of different combinations; once a combination settles, the later ones are trials of position, scale, and colour.

For Methods 1, does the final version count as one of the ten versions?

Yes.

Should my hybrid monster image be portrait or landscape?

Landscape. The brief asks for an image "at least 1920 x 960 in resolution, formatted in landscape (horizontal) orientation on a 11 x 17 in canvas, and saved in JPG file type."

Does the Methods 1 story have to be printed?

No. Upload the .docx to Canvas and be ready to tell it out loud in lab.

What can I choose as my Project 1 subject?

The brief asks for "a fabricated consumer product that is 1) light enough to be held in your hands, and 2) made of a singular material." Pick something that meets both conditions and is an object rather than a category, so "plastic" and "food waste" will not work.

Can I do an aluminium can as my Project 1 subject?

Yes. Note that the lecture follows one all the way through, so you will have heard a lot about it already.

Can I do a coffee cup as my Project 1 subject?

Yes. A lining does not disqualify a subject. What matters is that you can name one thing you will follow, and one path it takes after it leaves a person's hands. Aluminium works because it runs from the mine to the smelter to the furnace to the recycling rate to the deposit law, and the sources along the way are all about the same thing.

How do I know my Project 1 topic is too big or too complicated?

Three things make a subject hard to finish:

  • Too complex. A smartphone has batteries, a screen, and circuit boards, which is too many sub-elements to research properly.
  • Very little waste, and no real consumer decision. Car tires are used until they wear out; people rarely replace them on impulse.
  • Not everyday, and detached from consumers. Space junk.

Subjects that work well: canned food, canned beverages, plastic water bottles, garments, shopping bags, takeout boxes, beverage cups, shipping cardboard.

Someone else already took the Project 1 topic I wanted.

Choose another one, and your Learning Assistant can help you find one in lab. No more than four students across all sections can take the same topic. You register your topic with your Learning Assistant in lab on Thursday, September 3.

Which library modules do I have to do for Project 1, and when?

Both of them, before the research begins. They are Finding Articles using Academic Search Premier and Finding Reputable Sources on the Web, linked in the Project 1 brief.

What counts as a scholarly article?

Peer-reviewed work. The brief describes the clues: "they are text heavy and dense, authored by people affiliated with universities, and starts with an abstract." A blog, a newspaper article, or a social media post does not count. Two scholarly articles are the minimum.

How much of each article do I have to read for my Project 1 research?

More than the abstract. The brief says: "you should not read only the abstract or skip to only the conclusion at the very end." The results and discussion sections are where the specific data is. You also need at least one set of data clipped from your articles to use later as a data graph element.

Is the Project 1 research form one per article, or one for both?

One form per article, so two forms in total.

Is there a length requirement for the Project 1 research form?

No. What matters is that each section shows information you pulled out of the article.

What is the Project 1 research form for, and how does it relate to the research slideshow?

The form is for pulling information out of a paper. The slideshow presents what you found. The two will overlap a lot. In the form, do not rewrite, summarise, or evaluate the paper; extract and clip the information.

How long is the Project 1 research slideshow, per article or in total?

More than 10 and fewer than 20 slides in total. It is one slideshow covering both articles, not one per article.

What is the Project 1 written narrative for?

It pulls your research together into a thorough and clear account of the problem, so you have to select, structure, and synthesise rather than list. Check it against two questions: would an ordinary person already know this from intuition alone, and does a listener understand the problem more deeply after hearing it? It cannot repeat what everybody already knows about the material, and it cannot be invented from imagination or stereotype.

How does the Project 1 research connect to the image I make later?

The research decides what you want to say. The later parts are about how to say it clearly through the image.

What is composition? What are graphic precedents?

Those come in a later lecture. The Project 1 brief explains both, so read that first.

Do I use my earlier composition as the base for the eidetic image?

Yes, directly. Put it under your work in Photoshop with some opacity, or turn it on and off to check against it. Turn it off when you export.

My composition from Deliverable 4 is not working. Can I change the structure?

Yes. The brief asks you to use one of your five versions as the structure; it does not ask you to keep it unchanged. Change it if the image needs it, and be able to say what the change is for. If your precedent was a poor choice you can pick another, but make sure it is a composition and not a pattern.

Can I switch to a different one of my five Deliverable 4 versions?

Yes, any of the five works as a starting point. Make sure you use it as the structure you place things on.

Does my eidetic image have to look like the image on the Project 1 brief?

No. We want you to learn the method and apply it yourself. Do your own research and look at references, especially how they use the methods we discussed. Do not copy an image you already know.

How many images should be on my eidetic image?

At least 10–15 visual materials, matched to 10–15 key points or keywords from your written narrative. Avoid icons. An oil pumpjack icon, for example, loses everything about the land, the environment, and the visual presence of the problem.

Can I put numbers directly on my eidetic image?

Yes, as long as they are readable and handled as part of the composition.

Can I use a chart straight from one of my articles?

Yes, that is why you clipped it. Build it into the composition rather than pasting it on top. Use the same colour, cropping, and scale logic as everything else on the page.

How do I get my eidetic image file to the right size and resolution?

Set the document to 11x17 at 200 ppi when you start. Changing it later will not make a blurry image sharp. What is checked is whether any image on the page is blurry.

If something does go wrong, tell your Learning Assistant as early as you can so they can offer help or point you to the right resources. You may also opt to request your one-time extension per syllabus, however, that needs to be sent out and agreed upon 48 hours in advance.

What are the four individual iterations in Methods 2?

Same as in the earlier assignments: they build the habit and the ability to keep iterating on your work. As the brief says, they can be exploratory (different directions) or evolutionary (one direction developing). Each time you iterate, have a clear purpose, and decide what to change based on that purpose.

What are the group iterations in Methods 2?

Each of you makes four or more individual iterations first, and then the two of you make two or more combined iterations as a team. Deliverable 4 is one file showing that evolution, at least 10 versions in total, annotated to show individual and team work. The combined iterations are your first step in learning to create as a team. When the two of you bring different ideas to the table, you need to understand and evaluate each other's ideas, pick the stronger direction, and combine the meaningful ideas and content from both of you in a way that works. Using Miro well will help you iterate on the content together more effectively.

Which product do we map in Project 2?

Use a Project 1 product. If the two of you brought different products, pick one of them. If you have a compelling reason to change to something else, it still has to meet the Project 1 definition: "a fabricated consumer product that is 1) light enough to be held in your hands, and 2) made of a singular material."

What is the cradle-to-grave journey?

It is the physical life of your product. You follow its material from where it originates, through each place and process where it is transformed, to where it ends up as waste. Every fabricated product traces back to natural resources: minerals, plants, or other parts of the natural world.

This is not a timeline like Methods 2, and not the story of why the product came to exist. It is the material itself, tracked from start to end.

The brief describes this journey in broad stages: extraction, production, transportation, use and disposal. Treat those as the top level of your structure, not as your nodes. Under each one, work out the specific places and processes your material actually passes through, the way we built the structure in the lecture, and find out through your research (Deliverables 3a and 3b) what happens at each of them. That research is what takes your understanding beyond assumptions, stereotypes and general knowledge. Pay particular attention to the impact on the land and air at each step.

Pay close attention to how the journey ends, and do not assume it all goes to landfill or ends up harming some random marine life. Waste is handled in other ways as well, and a product can have several recovery paths: formal recycling channels, and social or community ones such as donation and charity collection. These paths usually carry some reward or circulation value, so find out what it is. When recycling sends the material back into a production cycle, the journey becomes cradle-to-cradle.

What am I actually researching for Project 2?

What you are mapping is that product's journey from cradle to grave, with particular attention to its impact on the land and air.

Build the structure before anyone reads anything. The lecture was about how to create a logical structure for what you are researching: how to layer it, and how to expand it by looking for the items that sit parallel to the ones you already have. For example, the top level may have three parts, make, use and discard. Each of those opens into several stages: make might cover raw material extraction, processing that material, manufacturing the product, and packaging it for transport. Under each stage sit the questions: where does it happen, who works there, what goes in and what comes out. Expanding each level is how you end up with the full set of nodes instead of only the ones you thought of first. As in Methods 2, you should end up with at least 10 nodes along the journey.

Then split them. If your structure comes out at eight main points, you take four each. Four articles each works out at roughly one article per point, and you fill the rest in with your own further checking. That is what gives each point enough depth to be worth putting on the map.

Read the brief all the way through. Reading and internalizing information is one of the abilities this course is building.

Where do we start on the Project 2 research?

First work together to settle the logical structure of the research: what the nodes actually are. Refer to the first-, second-, and third-level logical framework covered in class. The brief states it as a rule: "Do not start your individual research until the team has settled the structure and the nodes." Once the structure is settled, each of you does your own research and makes the form and slides.

How do the Project 2 research form and slides relate to the journey slides?

The form and slides work the same way as in Project 1: an organized way to store the information in the article and then present it. For Project 2 you individually read at least four academic journal articles and fill in four forms, one per article. The journey slides are where you reassemble that information according to your journey. You select and rearrange the information and pair it with suitable visuals, and those visuals lay the groundwork for your later image work.

What should be in the journey slides?

They are the journey map minus the mapping. Organize them by the structure your team built at the start: everyone's research assembled back into one cradle-to-grave journey and presented in that order, node by node. This is where the parts you each researched separately come back together as one account. One team file, due Tuesday, October 6, 22:00, and you will have class time on Tuesday to work on it.

For each node, assemble the information from your research to explain what happens there. Go beyond general information: specific facts and numbers from your articles, data charts or graphs where you have them, images where they help, and APA citations wherever you used an article. A node that reads "transportation: emissions" is not finished yet. Be sure to highlight the significant impacts on the land and the air at each step, explaining their causes, locations, extent, and severity.

Collect your images while you are making the slides. You will need visual material to explain the journey, and the same material can become material for your journey map. For each node, think about what should be shown, and look across different kinds: photographs, charts, diagrams, maps. Keep them as specific as you can to that place, that node and that process. Avoid generalized stand-ins such as a random factory, a random chimney, or an icon.

What is graphic research?

Graphic research means collecting graphic precedents with a clear intention for informing your subsequent work. For Project 2 the intention is that the images should help you think about how to build a linear reading structure, and how to host image information that will be more complex and richer than before.

How many graphic precedents do I select for Project 2?

Look at 20–30 on Pinterest, choose 3, print them on letter paper, and trace them with tracing paper before you move on to the iterations. Those iterations are three variations of your own composition, built from one of the three images you selected. Choose them for what they show you about building a linear reading structure and about holding image information that is more complex and richer than before. A precedent with too few elements will not give you enough to work from. Read the Project 2 brief carefully.

What comes after the journey slides in Project 2?

Same as Project 1: translate the text content into graphic elements. The finished journey map should have at least 15 visual materials, and more is better. Then you can start making the journey map based on the composition.

How many iterations does the Project 2 journey map need?

Three per person, plus four as a team. Each of you individually develops three iterations of the journey map, and then the team combines that work and creates four additional iterations together. All of them go in with Deliverable 6a, labelled to show which are individual and which are team, on Canvas with Deliverable 5b by Monday, October 12, 22:00.

How do we use the Miro board for Project 2?

Treat it as a virtual whiteboard among your group members. Use it to hold your research information and the case images you find. Later, when you make the journey slides and the journey map, you can use features like sticky notes to move and organize information. That way your partner can see what you are making and how you are organizing it.

What do we submit for Miro and the shared drive in Project 2?

Screenshots of the team's Miro board (one overview plus a few details) and a screenshot of the shared drive folder. They go in with Deliverable 6a on Canvas, in the Project 2 Final Submission, by Monday, October 12, 22:00. The purpose is to help you learn how to collaborate on creative work.
