Methods 1 — The Hybrid Monster

Does the final version count as one of the ten versions? Yes.

Do the ten versions mean ten files? No. The ten versions are laid out on one 11x17 sheet, submitted as a single file.

Do the ten versions have to be ten finished pieces? No. They record the process. Some of them are trials of different combinations; once a combination settles, the later ones are trials of position, scale, and colour.

Portrait or landscape? Landscape. The brief asks for an image "at least 1920 x 960 in resolution, formatted in landscape (horizontal) orientation on a 11 x 17 in canvas, and saved in JPG file type."

Does the natural object have to be an animal? No. The brief says "one of the objects should be artificially created, while the other should be a creature found in nature," and we are reading that more broadly: any natural object works, paired with a fabricated one. If you have already built yours around an animal, that is fine and nothing changes for you.

Does the story have to be printed? No. Upload the .docx to Canvas and be ready to tell it out loud in lab. Nothing about the story goes on paper.

Project 1 — Waste

What can I choose as a subject? The brief asks for "a fabricated consumer product that is 1) light enough to be held in your hands, and 2) made of a singular material." Both conditions have to hold, and it has to be an object rather than a category — "plastic" and "food waste" are not subjects.

Can I do an aluminium can? Yes. The lecture follows one all the way through, so you will have heard a lot about it already — worth asking yourself which angle you would take that the lecture did not.

Can I do a coffee cup? Yes. The single-material condition is looser than it sounds, and a cup is fine. A subject is not turned away for having a lining. What actually matters is whether the account closes: can you name one thing you will follow, and one path it takes after it leaves a person's hands? Aluminium closes, because it runs from the mine to the smelter to the furnace to the recycling rate to the deposit law and the sources along the way are all about the same thing.

How do I know my topic is too big or too complicated? Three things make a subject hard to finish:

  • Too complex. A smartphone has batteries, a screen, and circuit boards — too many sub-elements to research properly.
  • Very little waste, and no real consumer decision. Car tires are used until they wear out; people rarely replace them on impulse.
  • Not everyday, and detached from consumers. Space junk.

Subjects that work well: canned food, canned beverages, plastic water bottles, garments, shopping bags, takeout boxes, beverage cups, shipping cardboard.

Someone else already took my topic. Each topic can be taken by no more than four students across all sections. If your topic is already full, choose something else; your Learning Assistant can help you do it in lab. Topics are registered with your Learning Assistant in lab on Thursday, September 3.

Do I really have to do the library modules? Yes, both of them, and before the research begins. They are Finding Articles using Academic Search Premier and Finding Reputable Sources on the Web, linked in the Project 1 brief.

What counts as a scholarly article? Peer-reviewed work. The brief describes the clues: "they are text heavy and dense, authored by people affiliated with universities, and starts with an abstract." A blog, a newspaper article, or a social media post does not count. Two scholarly articles are the minimum.

How much of each paper do I have to read? More than the abstract. The brief is explicit: "you should not read only the abstract or skip to only the conclusion at the very end." The results and discussion sections are where the specific data lives, and you need at least one set of data clipped from your articles to use later as a data graph element.

How long is the research slideshow — per article, or in total? More than 10 and fewer than 20 slides in total. It is one slideshow covering both articles, not one per article.

Is the reading form one per article, or one for both? One form per article, so two forms in total.

Is there a length requirement for the reading form? No. What matters is that it shows information was pulled out of each section it asks about.

What is the reading form for, and how does it relate to the slideshow? The form is an internal tool for pulling information out of a paper. The slideshow is the outward-facing presentation of what you found. The content will overlap a lot, and that is normal. Do not rewrite, summarise, or evaluate the paper in the form — extract and clip the information.

What is the written narrative for? It pulls your research together into an account of the problem that is thorough and clear, which means you have to select, structure, and synthesise rather than list. The test: would an ordinary person already know this from intuition alone? After hearing it, does the listener understand the problem more deeply? It cannot be a repetition of what everybody already knows about the material, and it cannot be invented from imagination or stereotype.

How does this part connect to what comes later? This step decides what you want to say. The later parts are about how to say it clearly through the image.

What is composition? What are graphics precedents? Those come in a later lecture. Read the Project 1 brief first — it explains both.

Project 1 — The Eidetic Image

What do I submit on Friday, September 18? One .jpg on Canvas, 11x17 at 200 ppi or higher. It is one image — not a slideshow, and not the .psd.

How is the progress .psd graded? It is worth 5 points and is graded on progress and visible iterative development, not on how finished it looks. Reasonable progress gets full marks.

Do I need to print this week? No. The print is due in lab on Thursday, September 24, and it is the same image you submit on Friday.

Do I use my earlier composition as the base? Yes, directly. Put it under your work in Photoshop with some opacity, or turn it on and off to check against it. Turn it off when you export.

Does it have to look like the image on the Project 1 brief? No. We want you to learn the method and apply it yourself. Do your own research and look at references, especially how they use the methods we discussed. Do not try to copy an image you already know — work with the uncertainty as creative freedom.

My composition from Deliverable 4 is not working. Can I change the structure? Yes. The brief asks you to use one of your five versions as the structure; it does not ask you to keep it unchanged. Change it if the image needs it, and be able to say what the change is for. If your precedent was a poor choice you can pick another, but make sure it is a composition and not a pattern.

Can I switch to a different one of my five versions? Yes, any of the five is a legitimate starting point. Just make sure you use it as the structure you place things on.

How many images should be on it? At least 10–15 visual materials, matched to 10–15 key points or keywords from your written narrative. Avoid icons — an oil pumpjack icon, for example, loses everything about the land, the environment, and the visual presence of the problem.

Can I put numbers directly on the image? Yes, as long as they are readable and handled as part of the composition.

Can I use a chart straight from one of my articles? Yes, that is why you clipped it. Build it into the composition rather than pasting it on top — the same colour, cropping, and scale logic as everything else on the page.

How do I get the size right? Set the document to 11x17 at 200 ppi when you start. Changing it later will not make a blurry image sharp. What matters is whether any image on the page is blurry.

My file is too big, or Photoshop crashed. Save to a cloud drive, and use the computer lab if you need to. This does not change the deadline.

Can I get an extension on the final? An extension has to be requested at least 48 hours before the deadline and agreed by the instructor. For the Friday, September 18, 22:00 deadline, that means agreed by Wednesday, September 16, 22:00.

Will my work be shown next Tuesday? Selected Project 1 work is presented in lecture on Tuesday, September 22. Presenters are drawn at random and nominated by the teaching team, so be ready to talk about your product and the problem around it, and about how you built your image, including your iterations.

Methods 2 — Tracking and Visualizing a Journey

Which work do I present in Thursday's lab (September 24)? Your Project 1 eidetic image, the print you just pinned on the wall. Not Methods 2.

How does pairing work? Everyone gets a partner. We follow your preferences as far as we can. We suggest choosing classmates whose Project 1 topic roughly aligns with yours, and also thinking about each other's interests, ways of working, and availability.

When will I know who my partner is? Within 60 minutes of the end of lab.

Do I submit my pitch now? No. But before Thursday is over, set up a meeting with your partner, pitch to each other, and decide on your topic, so that you have enough working time afterwards. Submit it together with the rest on Monday, September 28, by 22:00.

What are the four individual iterations? Same as before: they build the habit and the ability to keep iterating on your work. As the brief says, they can be exploratory (different directions) or evolutionary (one direction developing). Each time you iterate, have a clear purpose, and decide what to change based on that purpose.

And what are the group iterations? They are your first step in learning to create as a team. When the two of you bring different ideas to the table, you need to understand and evaluate each other's ideas, pick the stronger direction, and combine the meaningful ideas and content from both of you in a way that works. Using Miro well will help you iterate on the content together more effectively.

Software

Adobe still says I am on a trial. When you sign in, choose the office or school account option, not the personal one. If it still says trial after that, go to Husker Tech.

Which Photoshop should I use? The desktop application, not the browser version. The interfaces differ and the browser version will not match what is demonstrated in lab. Then set your workspace to Graphic and Web under Window > Workspace, so that your screen matches everyone else's.

Printing

Where and how do I print? At the Media Center. The training video walks through it: unl.yuja.com/v/unlarchprinting

The queue was long and I could not get my print done. The syllabus is direct about this: "Several sessions require an 11x17 print brought to class, and all sections print on the same days. Send your print job at least a day ahead. A queue at the Media Center on the morning of a pin-up is not an accepted reason for arriving without work." Send the job the evening before.

Deadlines and grades

What happens if I submit late? From the syllabus: within 15 minutes of the deadline, a 15% deduction; within the first 24 hours, a 50% reduction; beyond 24 hours, a zero.

Can I get an extension? Once per semester. From the syllabus: "you are allowed one extension request, which will extend the deadline for one assignment for one week... Request needs to be made 48 hours or more from the deadline of the assignment and agreed upon by the instructor." Group work gets one group request, with every member agreeing. Your Learning Assistant cannot grant one.

Is there any reward for staying on time? Yes. From the syllabus: "If you submitted all of your assignments on time, did not use the extension request... and received at least a passing grade for each assignment, then by the end of the semester, you will receive a 3% increase in your overall grade."

Where is my grade? Grades stay hidden in Canvas while they are entered and calibrated across sections. The goal is to post within two weeks of the due date.

Will my work be shown at a Tuesday session? Possibly. From the syllabus: "Presenters for these are drawn at random and nominated by the teaching team, so plan on the possibility that your work will be shown and be ready to speak to it. Being selected is not extra credit, and not being selected is not a penalty."
